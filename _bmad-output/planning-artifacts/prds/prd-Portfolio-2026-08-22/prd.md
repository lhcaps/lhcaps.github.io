# PRD: Le Huy Systems Atlas

## 0. Document purpose

This PRD is the product contract for UX, architecture, story creation, implementation, review, and release of Le Huy Systems Atlas. It defines the reader journeys, exact product vocabulary, grouped capabilities, stable Functional Requirement (FR) IDs, quality boundaries, and release outcomes. The product brief lives in `../../briefs/brief-Portfolio-2026-08-22/`; implementation constraints and discovery-state evidence live in this workspace's `addendum.md`.

## 1. Vision

Le Huy Systems Atlas is an English-language engineering portfolio for a full-time Junior Software Engineer target: roughly 60% full-stack product engineering and 40% backend depth, with backend architecture as the strongest proof. It lets a recruiter identify the candidate and act within one viewport, then lets a technical reader trace the difficult part of each system: where authority changes, where work crosses a runtime, where requirements revise a pricing rule, where low-confidence automation hands control to a person, and where a verification gate rejects stale evidence.

Concrete parts stand for the engineer. A DOCX Contract stands for governance. A Queue Handoff stands for cross-runtime architecture. A Fourth Pricing Change stands for adaptation. A Manual Review Handoff stands for operational judgment. A Failed Gate stands for verification discipline. The portfolio earns three conclusions without reducing them to slogans: Le Huy finishes difficult systems, adapts when requirements move, and is trustworthy and enjoyable to work with.

The portfolio is itself a verified system. One meaningful Systems Atlas makes architecture explorable; every material public Claim remains traceable to Public-Safe Evidence; semantic alternatives preserve the same meaning without WebGL; and the release is complete only when the final exact SHA passes the full Verification Harness on GitHub Pages. The Primary Action is **Work with me**.

Repository truth and Public-Safe Evidence govern implementation facts and Claims. This PRD and its approved downstream contracts govern product behavior. Correctness, accessibility, privacy, and verification outrank visual preference whenever those authorities conflict.

## 2. Target readers

### 2.1 Jobs to be done

- A recruiter can identify Le Huy, the target role, the backend-leaning full-stack orientation, the three Flagship Systems, and the Primary Action before deciding whether to continue.
- An engineering interviewer can compare five System Topologies, inspect decisions and failure modes, and distinguish implemented source from runtime or production proof.
- A potential collaborator can understand how Le Huy discovers, implements, verifies, and releases work, then reach a direct contact path.
- A privacy-conscious stakeholder can understand the Adaptation Loop without seeing the confidential client's identity, pricing, screenshots, configuration, payment details, or physical-room identity.
- A reader on a small screen, using Reduced Motion, using only a keyboard, or without WebGL can access the same systems and relationships.

### 2.2 Non-readers for this release

- Visitors looking for a blog, chronological repository mirror, tutorial library, or exhaustive technology inventory.
- Visitors looking for confidential client documentation, operational credentials, production topology, or business metrics.
- Content-management authors; this release has no CMS or editorial workflow.

### 2.3 Key reader journeys

- **UJ-1. Maya decides whether Le Huy fits the role.**
  - **Persona + context:** Maya is a recruiter reviewing several junior software-engineering candidates between meetings.
  - **Entry state:** She opens the public portfolio on a laptop with no prior context.
  - **Path:** She reads Le Huy's name and role, sees the backend-leaning proposition, follows the Primary Action or scans the three Flagship Systems, and checks the Closing for GitHub, email, and CV.
  - **Climax:** She can state what Le Huy builds and why the work is credible without decoding a technology inventory.
  - **Resolution:** She opens a verified contact or CV destination, or continues into a Flagship Narrative.

- **UJ-2. Aaron tests the architecture story.**
  - **Persona + context:** Aaron is an engineering interviewer deciding whether the visible work reflects real system judgment.
  - **Entry state:** He enters at the Opening or follows a direct link to Selected Systems.
  - **Path:** He uses the System Selector by keyboard or pointer, compares System Topologies, reads a Flagship Narrative, follows the Adaptation Loop, then checks the Verification Harness and Evidence Boundary.
  - **Climax:** He sees a concrete decision, its constraint, its handoff or failure mode, and the evidence level that supports the public Claim.
  - **Resolution:** He leaves with specific interview questions instead of an unqualified technology list.

- **UJ-3. Priya checks the confidential boundary.**
  - **Persona + context:** Priya is a stakeholder assessing whether client work is presented responsibly.
  - **Entry state:** She opens the Production Booking & Operations Platform narrative.
  - **Path:** She follows the Fourth Pricing Change through feedback, rule, implementation, verification, release, and next revision; she checks what the page deliberately withholds.
  - **Climax:** The Adaptation Loop remains technically useful while revealing no client identity, price, screenshot, credential, provider configuration, payment detail, sensitive architecture, or physical-room identity.
  - **Resolution:** She can trust the publication boundary without needing access to the private Evidence Ledger.

- **UJ-4. Sam explores without the focal scene.**
  - **Persona + context:** Sam uses a narrow mobile viewport with Reduced Motion enabled; the browser may also lack working WebGL.
  - **Entry state:** Sam reaches the Systems Atlas after the Opening.
  - **Path:** Sam uses the Readable Equivalent Representation, changes the Selected System by touch or keyboard, reads relationships and state changes, and continues to the same Selected Systems content.
  - **Climax:** Every relationship needed to understand the Selected System is available without continuous motion, hover, or WebGL.
  - **Resolution:** Sam completes the same content path without a scroll trap, hidden information, or broken layout.

## 3. Glossary

- **Adaptation Loop** — The anonymous sequence Feedback → Pricing Rule → Implementation → Verification → Release → Next Revision.
- **Claim** — A material public statement about a System, implementation, verification result, or release state.
- **Closing** — The final contact region containing the Primary Action, GitHub, email, and verified CV.
- **Evidence Boundary** — The rule that each Claim uses only Public-Safe Evidence at its observed evidence level and never exposes the private Evidence Ledger.
- **Evidence Ledger** — The private, unpublished record of a Claim, repository, source, observed implementation, evidence classification, confidence, public-safety decision, and limitation. It contains one evidence file per System.
- **Failed Gate** — A current or historical verification result shown with its exact failure qualification, never presented as a passing result.
- **Flagship Narrative** — A deep, system-specific account of a trigger, constraint, decision, mechanism, failure mode, verification, result, or lesson. The three Flagship Systems each use a distinct narrative shape.
- **Flagship System** — Form Management, VisionFlow Studio, or Production Booking & Operations Platform.
- **Fourth Pricing Change** — The public-safe anchor for the anonymous Production Booking & Operations Platform's requirement-adaptation story; it refers to the fourth reviewable pricing-model revision, not confidential pricing data.
- **Manual Review Handoff** — The Parkly transition that sends a low-confidence capture to a person for a decision.
- **Primary Action** — The exact call to action **Work with me**.
- **Public Claim Manifest** — The publishable validation contract derived from the private Evidence Ledger. Each entry has a stable evidence key, System ID, Claim scope, evidence classification, public-safe status, and required limitation; it contains no private source detail.
- **Public-Safe Evidence** — Evidence approved for public use after source, evidence level, confidence, confidentiality, and limitation review.
- **Queue Handoff** — The VisionFlow Studio transition in which a job leaves the HTTP request path for asynchronous worker processing.
- **Readable Equivalent Representation** — A semantic DOM, text, CSS, or SVG presentation that exposes the same System selection, nodes, routes, relationships, and state meaning as the focal Systems Atlas scene.
- **Reduced Motion** — A reader preference that preserves state and sequence while removing continuous or nonessential movement.
- **Selected System** — The one System currently in focus in the Systems Atlas and its Readable Equivalent Representation.
- **Supporting Narrative** — A concise, evidence-bounded account for a Supporting System; it is not a compressed copy of a Flagship Narrative.
- **Supporting System** — Parkly or TFT Local Copilot.
- **System** — One of the five curated engineering projects represented by content and topology data.
- **System Selector** — The pointer-, touch-, and keyboard-operable control that changes the Selected System.
- **System Topology** — The deterministic, evidence-supported nodes, routes, layers, labels, and state mappings for one System.
- **Systems Atlas** — The single guided and explorable architecture visualization that reconfigures among all five System Topologies.
- **Verification Harness** — The visible product narrative and executable release checks that distinguish authored output from accepted evidence.

## 4. Features

### 4.1 Opening and navigation

**Description:** The Opening gives Maya the minimum complete identity and proposition, while navigation provides direct, truthful routes to each major section. It realizes UJ-1 and provides the entry point for UJ-2.

#### FR-1: Establish identity and proposition

The portfolio presents Le Huy, **Software Engineer**, a Junior Software Engineer target, and a backend-leaning full-stack proposition in the Opening.

**Consequences (testable):**

- The first rendered viewport contains the candidate name, role, orientation, a concrete system-led proposition, and the Primary Action at every defined release viewport where layout permits without overlap.
- The Opening contains no portrait, avatar, human silhouette, eyebrow label, availability pill, technology inventory, fake metric, fake terminal, or unsupported scale claim.
- The Opening remains meaningful before the Systems Atlas chunk loads or when that chunk fails.

#### FR-2: Provide reliable navigation and action paths

The reader can navigate to the Systems Atlas, Selected Systems, Adaptation Loop, AI-assisted engineering, Verification Harness, capabilities, and Closing, and can activate the Primary Action. Realizes UJ-1 and UJ-2.

**Consequences (testable):**

- Every navigation destination resolves to one unique existing section ID and remains operable by keyboard and touch.
- Focus is visible after keyboard navigation; no navigation control depends on hover alone.
- Mobile navigation exposes its state accessibly, closes predictably, and does not trap focus or scrolling.
- The Primary Action uses the exact label **Work with me** and resolves to a verified contact destination.

### 4.2 Systems Atlas

**Description:** One Systems Atlas lets Aaron compare architectural differences without turning the page into a decorative 3D toy. Depth, routes, focus, selection, and motion carry documented meaning. The Readable Equivalent Representation is a first-class presentation of the same data. Realizes UJ-2 and UJ-4.

#### FR-3: Select all five Systems from one model

The System Selector changes the Selected System among exactly five Systems: Form Management, VisionFlow Studio, Production Booking & Operations Platform, Parkly, and TFT Local Copilot.

**Consequences (testable):**

- Pointer, touch, Enter, and Space selection expose the same five Systems and selected state.
- The selected control is programmatically determinable, and selection moves focus or announces change without disorienting the reader.
- One authoritative data model supplies the System Selector, System Topology, Readable Equivalent Representation, and related narrative references; validators reject missing or duplicated System IDs and unresolved routes.

#### FR-4: Give every topology element meaning

Each System Topology encodes evidence-supported architectural layers, subsystem ownership, routes, dependencies, handoffs, or focus context; Atlas motion expresses a state transition rather than ambience.

**Consequences (testable):**

- Every node and route has a semantic label and a readable relationship in the authoritative data model.
- Depth, link style, focus, selected state, and each motion class have a documented meaning available outside WebGL.
- Project change may assemble, route, focus, reveal, resolve, compare, hand off, collapse, or reconnect; perpetual spinning, arbitrary particles, starfields, bloom, unrestricted orbit controls, unrelated shader noise, and unmapped glow are absent.
- Selecting a visible node or route either performs a documented action or is not presented as interactive.

#### FR-5: Preserve meaning without WebGL or continuous motion

The portfolio presents a Readable Equivalent Representation whenever WebGL is unavailable, errors, is inappropriate for the viewport, has not loaded, or is intentionally bypassed. Realizes UJ-4.

**Consequences (testable):**

- The Readable Equivalent Representation exposes the Selected System, its nodes, ordered or connected routes, layer meaning, and system-selection controls.
- Reduced Motion preserves system state and transition sequence without continuous animation.
- Mobile may use different geometry or a non-3D composition but preserves System Topology meaning and access to all five Systems.
- A scene error is contained and replaced with readable content; no public Claim or navigation path exists only inside WebGL.

#### FR-6: Keep the focal scene out of the critical path

The Systems Atlas reserves stable layout space and loads its focal scene after the essential Opening content.

**Consequences (testable):**

- The initial page renders identity, proposition, Primary Action, and a usable Atlas fallback without waiting for the 3D implementation.
- The release contains one focal Canvas and no competing decorative Canvas.
- Loading, failure, and fallback states do not cause a material layout jump or leave an empty region.

### 4.3 Selected Systems

**Description:** Selected Systems turns each concrete anchor into a different kind of engineering proof. Three Flagship Narratives carry depth; two Supporting Narratives add breadth without repeated card anatomy. Realizes UJ-1, UJ-2, and UJ-3.

#### FR-7: Publish three distinct Flagship Narratives

The portfolio presents deep narratives for Form Management, VisionFlow Studio, and Production Booking & Operations Platform, with each narrative shaped by the system's strongest verified anchor.

**Consequences (testable):**

- Form Management uses the DOCX Contract and governance boundary to explain typed contracts, identity versus authorization, preview versus persisted output, audit, and a precisely qualified Failed Gate.
- VisionFlow Studio uses the Queue Handoff to explain deterministic ingestion, locked dataset versions, controlled pipelines, asynchronous processing, guarded inference, and deterministic evaluation without claiming fresh service runtime proof.
- Production Booking & Operations Platform uses the Fourth Pricing Change and Adaptation Loop while preserving the Evidence Boundary.
- Each Flagship Narrative includes a useful subset of trigger, constraint, decision, mechanism, failure mode, verification, result, and lesson; no rigid shared template forces all three into the same rhythm.

#### FR-8: Publish two honest Supporting Narratives

The portfolio presents concise Supporting Narratives for Parkly and TFT Local Copilot.

**Consequences (testable):**

- Parkly uses the Manual Review Handoff to explain operational state and human review; it calls the lock a custom Redis ownership lock, never Redlock, and does not invent a direct event link from manual review to the separate outbox delivery rail.
- TFT Local Copilot is presented as an experiment with source, ingestion, embedding, vector store, retrieval, local model, and stream components; it does not claim a working end-to-end RAG path while route-registration and migration gaps remain.
- Supporting Narratives remain shorter than Flagship Narratives and do not imitate their full structure.

#### FR-9: Enforce the Evidence Boundary for public copy

Every material Claim in Selected Systems, the Systems Atlas, the Adaptation Loop, and the Verification Harness maps to one Public-Safe Evidence entry before release.

**Consequences (testable):**

- A content validator rejects Claims without a valid evidence key, allowed public status, evidence level, and limitation decision.
- Before public case-study copy is written, the Evidence Ledger contains one private file per System and classifies evidence as `VERIFIED_IMPLEMENTED`, `VERIFIED_LOCAL`, `VERIFIED_TESTED`, `DOCUMENTED_ONLY`, `PLANNED`, `UNVERIFIED`, or `PRIVATE_DO_NOT_PUBLISH`.
- The Public Claim Manifest is the validator input and minimum public contract. Stable evidence keys survive copy movement; each key identifies one Claim scope, System ID, evidence classification, public-safe status, and required limitation. The private Evidence Ledger is its non-deployed review source, never the validator's runtime input.
- Source inspection, local verification, runtime observation, and production observation remain distinct evidence levels in public wording.
- The private Evidence Ledger is absent from the generated site, build output, repository history, and deployed artifact.
- No Claim invents employer relationships, user counts, scale, performance, uptime, awards, testimonials, business outcomes, or production status.

### 4.4 Anonymous Adaptation Loop

**Description:** The Adaptation Loop shows how a client requirement moved through implementation and release at least four reviewable times, using the Fourth Pricing Change as the representative part. It realizes UJ-3.

#### FR-10: Explain requirement change without revealing the client

The portfolio presents Feedback → Pricing Rule → Implementation → Verification → Release → Next Revision as a readable, ordered adaptation story.

**Consequences (testable):**

- The story explains what kind of rule changed and how the engineering loop responded, without publishing real prices or client-specific architecture.
- The public title remains **Production Booking & Operations Platform** unless a stricter confidentiality review requires a more generic title.
- Client identity, screenshots, credentials, provider configuration, payment detail, sensitive deployment information, and physical-room identity are absent.
- The story distinguishes source and Git evidence from live-production proof.

### 4.5 AI-assisted engineering and Verification Harness

**Description:** These sections make the engineering control loop visible. Tools are secondary to contracts, checks, review, and evidence. They realize UJ-2 and support UJ-1.

#### FR-11: Present AI-assisted engineering as a controlled loop

The portfolio presents Intent → Research → Product Brief → PRD → UX Contracts → Spec → Architecture → Story → Scoped Implementation → Harness → Review → Browser QA → Release Evidence under the statement **THE AGENT WRITES. THE HARNESS DECIDES.**

**Consequences (testable):**

- The narrative identifies the boundary between generated work and accepted work.
- The narrative explains that context engineering and BMAD contracts constrain each scoped implementation slice, deterministic gates accept or reject it, and browser QA plus release evidence close the loop.
- Tool or model names, if present, support the process explanation and never replace the engineering outcome.
- The section does not use a fake terminal, chatbot transcript, logo cloud, or unsupported productivity metric.

#### FR-12: Show verification through real gates

The Verification Harness explains repeatable checks and uses only current or exactly qualified historical results.

**Consequences (testable):**

- The section covers content/topology validation, type checking, linting, unit tests, production build, browser paths, accessibility, links, console, and deployment attestation as applicable release categories.
- A Failed Gate is labeled with the condition and limitation that made it fail; a stale or nonzero result is never styled or worded as passing.
- Release copy is generated or reviewed against fresh gate evidence so stale counts cannot silently survive.

### 4.6 Capabilities and Closing

**Description:** Capabilities synthesize the work by engineering outcome; the Closing gives Maya and collaborators direct next actions. Realizes UJ-1 and supports UJ-2.

The portfolio groups capabilities around outcomes such as governed domain contracts, asynchronous boundaries, deterministic data flows, operational handoffs, requirement adaptation, and release verification.

**Consequences (testable):**

- The section contains no logo cloud, skill-percentage meter, generic pill inventory, or unsupported expertise level.

#### FR-14: Provide verified contact and CV destinations

The Closing presents the Primary Action, GitHub, email, and a facts-only CV assembled from verified public evidence.

**Consequences (testable):**

- GitHub, email, Primary Action, and CV destinations are present, reachable by keyboard, and pass link validation.

### 4.7 Publication and release identity

**Description:** The public document must identify itself correctly in search, link previews, assistive technology, and GitHub Pages. It supports every journey.

#### FR-15: Publish complete metadata and machine-readable identity

The release publishes verified title, description, canonical URL, Open Graph metadata, Twitter metadata, favicon assets, sitemap, robots policy, and Person/ProfilePage structured data.

**Consequences (testable):**

#### FR-16: Verify and attest the final release

The repository provides one canonical verification entry point that composes current applicable checks, and GitHub Pages deploys only after those checks pass.

**Consequences (testable):**

- The verification entry point covers typecheck, lint, unit tests, production build, E2E, content/topology harness, accessibility, responsive overflow, console, links, metadata, and other repository-applicable gates without duplicating their logic.
- The README documents the actual architecture, Systems Atlas constraints, local development, verification, accessibility, performance, and GitHub Pages deployment; the public page remains the narrative rather than an implementation manual.

- Product, visual, motion, simplicity, code, and live screenshot reviews have no unresolved material findings.

- Release evidence preserves exit codes and test counts where available, and labels each reported statement as verified, observed, inferred, or blocked.
- If any required gate or exact-SHA attestation is incomplete, the release status is **PARTIAL**, never **VERIFIED_COMPLETE**.

## 5. Cross-cutting non-functional requirements

### 5.1 Accessibility and interaction

- The release targets [WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) for the complete page and all interaction paths.
- Semantic headings, landmarks, lists, buttons, and links expose the complete content order outside WebGL.
- All interactive behavior supports keyboard and touch, visible focus, accessible names, and the WCAG 2.2 Level AA target-size minimum.
- Text contrast is at least `4.5:1`, large text contrast is at least `3:1`, and meaningful non-text interface contrast is at least `3:1`, subject only to the standard's stated exceptions.
- No information depends on hover alone; no animation creates a scroll trap; no focus is placed inside an inaccessible Canvas surface.
- Reduced Motion removes continuous and nonessential motion while preserving selection, relationship, and sequence.
- Automated checks are paired with manual keyboard, focus, contrast, content-order, and screen-size review.

### 5.2 Responsive composition

- Mobile is a deliberate composition, not a uniformly scaled desktop layout.
- Content order, Selected System meaning, and Primary Action remain stable across viewport changes.
- The release has no unintended horizontal page overflow, clipped focus indicator, obscured anchor target, or unreadable text at the defined matrix and intermediate widths.
- Constrained-height layouts keep navigation, System Selector, and Primary Action reachable without sticky-element collisions.

### 5.3 Performance and rendering

- Essential Opening content and the Primary Action remain outside the 3D critical path.
- The architecture owner defines numeric initial-JavaScript and lazy-3D-chunk budgets, measurement units, and a major-asset inventory in the architecture contract before the first implementation story begins. A missing budget or measured excess is a release failure until the architecture contract explicitly resolves it.
- Device pixel ratio is controlled, expensive effects and large assets require an information-bearing reason, and offscreen rendering pauses or reduces activity.
- Frame-loop work does not trigger React render churn; loading and failure paths remain deterministic.

### 5.4 Content and evidence quality

- `DESIGN.md` and `EXPERIENCE.md` are the approved visual and behavioral spines. Together they must make the experience cinematic, playful, and minimal through system-derived framing, tactile interaction, observational humor, and restraint; every retained visual or motion treatment maps to a System, contract, flow, evidence state, adaptation state, verification state, or Le Huy's voice.
- Public text is English, energetic, concrete, direct, technically literate, and human.
- Concrete artifacts and handoffs carry personality through synecdoche; technical requirements and evidence qualifications remain literal.
- Public text uses active verbs and concrete nouns, gives each sentence one job, and avoids litotes, irony, sarcasm, corporate filler, generic portfolio language, and AI clichés.
- Content passes when each major section contains a verified concrete artifact, handoff, failure, revision, or transition; prohibited language is absent; any humor is observational, sincere, and non-ironic; and both prose review and live screenshot review have no material finding. This review must preserve an approachable collaboration signal without substituting generic personality adjectives.
- Every Claim survives content, confidentiality, evidence-level, and limitation review before build.

### 5.5 Privacy and release safety

- No secret, credential, token, private ledger, confidential client artifact, or sensitive configuration enters source control, build output, logs, screenshots, or deployed assets.
- External links use appropriate security attributes and do not expose hidden referrer-sensitive data.
- Repository history maintenance may remove only explicitly authorized AI co-author trailers after backup and identity safeguards; it never changes file trees, commit order, dates, or genuine human authorship.

### 5.6 Reliability and compatibility

- The portfolio remains readable and navigable when JavaScript loads slowly, the 3D chunk fails, WebGL is unavailable, or Reduced Motion is enabled.
- Automated tests use deterministic fixtures and topology data; visual review complements rather than replaces behavioral checks.
- GitHub Pages base paths, direct loads, internal anchors, and static assets work on the deployed origin.

## 6. Non-goals

- A portrait, avatar, generated person, silhouette, or 3D human.
- A black-neon or cyberpunk theme, Apple imitation, glassmorphism showcase, gradient branding, decorative grid, starfield, fake dashboard, fake terminal, logo cloud, bento-by-default composition, or perpetual ambient animation.
- A theme switcher, localization framework, CMS, blog, testimonial system, analytics product, or speculative reusable case-study framework.
- A second Canvas, decorative Rive animation, particle field, bloom pass, toy orbit control, or 3D element without documented information mapping.
- Confidential client disclosure or a claim that source inspection proves current runtime or production state.
- Dependency or library churn solely to satisfy a design preference.
- Brittle full-page pixel snapshots as the primary behavior or visual acceptance mechanism.
- Optimization for visit duration, click count, technology-name density, or visual-effect count.

## 8. Success metrics

### Primary

- **SM-1: Opening completeness.** All required identity signals in FR-1 and the Primary Action in FR-2 are present and unobscured at every defined release viewport. Validates FR-1 and FR-2.
- **SM-2: Atlas equivalence.** Five of five Systems pass topology validation and expose the same selected state and relationship meaning through the Systems Atlas and Readable Equivalent Representation. Validates FR-3 through FR-6.
- **SM-3: Claim integrity.** Every material public Claim has an allowed Public-Safe Evidence mapping, and zero private or unsupported Claims enter the build. Validates FR-7 through FR-12.
- **SM-4: Verified release.** All current applicable gates pass, no material review finding remains open, and GitHub Pages serves the final exact SHA. Validates FR-15 and FR-16.

### Secondary

- **SM-5: Interaction coverage.** Navigation, System selection, Primary Action, GitHub, email, and CV pass pointer, touch, keyboard, link, console, and overflow checks across the release matrix. Validates FR-2, FR-3, FR-5, and FR-14.
- **SM-6: Production report completeness.** The release record reports the initial JavaScript payload, lazy 3D chunk, major assets, production build result, and deployment SHA. Validates FR-6 and FR-16.

### Counter-metrics

- **SM-C1: Do not optimize visit duration or click count.** A recruiter reaching a useful conclusion quickly is a successful outcome; this counterbalances SM-1 and SM-5.
- **SM-C2: Do not optimize visual-effect count or Canvas usage.** One meaningful scene is the maximum default, not a minimum; this counterbalances SM-2.
- **SM-C3: Do not optimize technology-name density.** Mechanisms and outcomes outrank logo or keyword volume; this counterbalances SM-3.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Product impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3D becomes decoration or blocks content | The signature element weakens trust and excludes readers | Enforce FR-4 through FR-6, one Canvas, documented semantics, and Readable Equivalent Representation acceptance |
| Public copy outruns evidence | Technical readers receive a false signal; confidential work may leak | Enforce FR-9, private ledger exclusion, evidence-level wording, and confidentiality review before build |
| Five systems flatten into repeated cards | Distinct engineering decisions become interchangeable | Use distinct Flagship Narratives, shorter Supporting Narratives, and system-specific anchors |
| Portfolio process overwhelms the candidate | The site reads as tool theater | Keep tool names secondary, show the control loop once, and organize capabilities by outcome |
| Brownfield failures survive release | GitHub Pages serves a stale or broken artifact | Make the canonical verification entry point and exact-SHA attestation hard release gates |
| Missing or invented CV facts | Closing loses trust | Produce a facts-only CV from verified public evidence; omit any unverifiable category rather than fill it |
