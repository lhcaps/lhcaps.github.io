# PRD Quality Review — Le Huy Systems Atlas

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, decision-ready chain-top PRD. It pairs a distinctive portfolio thesis with explicit evidence, confidentiality, accessibility, performance, and exact-SHA release boundaries; the four previously material downstream gaps now have concrete governing contracts. No rubric finding adds useful information at this stage.

## Decision-readiness — strong

§1 states the central decisions directly: one meaningful Systems Atlas, Public-Safe Evidence, a semantic alternative to WebGL, and exact-SHA GitHub Pages verification. FR-16 makes the release gate unambiguous—an incomplete gate or attestation is **PARTIAL**, never **VERIFIED_COMPLETE**—while §10 supplies a concrete reopening condition when implementation challenges a Claim, confidentiality boundary, or release requirement.

## Substance over theater — strong

The PRD earns its differentiation through system-specific anchors: the DOCX Contract, Queue Handoff, Fourth Pricing Change, Manual Review Handoff, and Failed Gate in §1, each carried into FR-7 through FR-12. The four named protagonists in §2 are load-bearing rather than decorative, and §5.4 requires every retained visual or motion treatment to map to a System, contract, flow, evidence state, adaptation state, verification state, or Le Huy's voice.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The product thesis remains consistent from §1 through §8: enable readers to inspect real system judgment without overstating evidence or disclosing confidential work. The capabilities, scope, risks, and success metrics all serve that thesis; SM-2 and SM-3 measure topology equivalence and claim integrity, while SM-C1 through SM-C3 explicitly reject engagement, visual-effect, and technology-keyword theater.

## Done-ness clarity — strong

FR-1 through FR-16 have testable consequences, and addendum §5 turns release verification into a concrete viewport, state, and release-record matrix. The former cross-cutting ambiguity is resolved: §5.1 targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA and states `4.5:1`, `3:1`, and non-text `3:1` contrast bounds; §5.3 assigns numeric initial-JavaScript and lazy-3D budgets, measurement units, and major-asset inventory to the architecture owner before the first implementation story, with missing or exceeded budgets a release failure; and §5.4 gives content a pass checklist plus prose and live-screenshot review gates.

## Scope honesty — strong

§6 and §7 expressly exclude common portfolio expansion paths—CMS, localization, analytics, extra case studies, private disclosure, decorative rendering, and dependency churn—rather than leaving them implied. §11 contains no unsupported assumptions, and §10 ensures discoveries that invalidate the stated evidence or privacy position reopen the contract instead of becoming silent de-scopes.

## Downstream usability — strong

§3 supplies consistent domain definitions; UJ-1 through UJ-4 have named protagonists; FR-1 through FR-16 and SM-1 through SM-6 are contiguous and cross-referenced; and addendum §3 provides deterministic topology directions. The Public Claim Manifest now closes the evidence-contract gap: its §3 definition and FR-9 specify a stable evidence key, System ID, Claim scope, evidence classification, public-safe status, required limitation, the private-ledger derivation path, and its role as validator input without deploying private details.

## Shape fit — strong

This public, UX-significant brownfield portfolio feeds UX, architecture, stories, implementation, and release, and its shape matches that role. It uses four relevant reader journeys, differentiates three deep Flagship Narratives from two concise Supporting Narratives, preserves brownfield evidence limits in addendum §2, and confines MVP scope to a single-page, five-System portfolio.

## Mechanical notes

FR-1 through FR-16, UJ-1 through UJ-4, and SM-1 through SM-6 are contiguous and unique; cited ranges resolve. The glossary consistently names the five Systems, their public anchors, and the evidence vocabulary. There are no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` or `[NOTE FOR PM]` tags, matching the empty assumptions index and §10’s explicit reopen trigger.
