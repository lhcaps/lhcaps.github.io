# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary readers are recruiters and hiring managers who need Le Huy's target role, backend-leaning full-stack orientation, strongest systems, and contact path within a short scan. Engineering managers and interviewers need architecture, constraints, failure modes, verification, and honest evidence boundaries. Potential collaborators need a human voice, delivery habits, public work, and a direct **Work with me** action. Privacy-conscious stakeholders must be able to understand the anonymous client adaptation story without learning confidential details.

## Product Purpose

Le Huy Systems Atlas is an English-language portfolio for a full-time Junior Software Engineer target: roughly 60% full-stack product engineering and 40% backend depth. It uses one explorable architecture atlas, three flagship narratives, two supporting narratives, an anonymous adaptation loop, and a verification story to make backend-heavy work visible. Success means a first-time reader can identify the candidate and act, a technical reader can trace real system decisions, every material claim stays inside its evidence level, and GitHub Pages serves the final exact SHA only after all required gates pass.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, playful, minimal. The voice is energetic, concrete, direct, technically literate, and human. Precise observational humor may appear when it comes from a real engineering condition. Concrete artifacts carry personality: a DOCX contract stands for governance, a queue handoff stands for cross-runtime architecture, a fourth pricing change stands for adaptation, a manual-review handoff stands for operational judgment, and a failed gate stands for verification discipline. The resulting impression should be capable, approachable, trustworthy, and enjoyable to work with.

## Anti-references

- Generic developer portfolios built from technology inventories, logo clouds, skill pills, repeated cards, fake metrics, or chronological GitHub mirrors.
- Black-neon hacker interfaces, cyberpunk effects, fake terminals, fake dashboards, decorative grids, gradient branding, glassmorphism, Apple imitation, and bento-by-default layouts.
- Decorative 3D: starfields, arbitrary particles, spinning objects, unrestricted toy orbit controls, holograms, bloom, shader noise, or glow without documented state meaning.
- Portraits, avatars, silhouettes, generated people, testimonial theater, invented scale, and unsupported production claims.
- Perpetual ambient movement, hover-only information, scroll hijacking, and any Canvas that competes with content.
- Corporate filler, generic portfolio language, AI clichés, irony, sarcasm, and litotes.

## Design Principles

1. **Let the part carry the system.** Lead each story with one verified artifact, handoff, revision, or failure that reveals a larger engineering quality.
2. **Make depth earn its pixels.** The Systems Atlas uses depth, routes, focus, selection, and motion only when they explain topology or state.
3. **Proof precedes presentation.** Repository truth, public-safe evidence, correctness, accessibility, privacy, and verification outrank visual preference.
4. **Guide first, then invite exploration.** Every reader gets a clear default path; keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, mobile, and non-WebGL paths preserve the same meaning.
5. **Make motion finite and causal.** Movement assembles, routes, focuses, reveals, resolves, compares, hands off, collapses, or reconnects. Nothing moves merely to look alive.
6. **Keep the human close to the mechanism.** Direct copy and tactile feedback make the engineering precise without making the person remote.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The complete responsive page and interaction paths target WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Semantic DOM content carries every public claim and relationship outside WebGL. Keyboard and touch access, visible focus, target size, contrast, meaningful order, reduced motion, mobile-specific composition, scene error containment, and a readable non-WebGL equivalent are release requirements. No information depends on hover, color, continuous motion, or Canvas alone.
